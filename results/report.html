RuFaS-gym · Experiment 1 · 7-year horizon

An optimized ration earns +36% more than the farm's — by spending less on feed, not making more milk

Wrapping the validated RuFaS dairy simulator as an optimization environment, CMA-ES searches over the herd's ration to maximize profit. Against the farm's own configured ration it finds $761,844 more profit over 7 years — and every dollar of it comes from cheaper feeding at essentially unchanged milk.

How the number was derived

Four steps, each traceable to a column in the run's output — no figure here is hand-drawn or hand-typed.

1

A profit reward built on RuFaS's own economics

The reward for a stretch of simulated time is milk revenue − feed cost. Feed cost is RuFaS's figure: FeedManager.purchase_feed prices every purchase against the scenario's cost data and reports it. Milk revenue is the one external input — total milk (kg) times a market milk price of $0.45/kg. Because the price only scales revenue, which ration is cheaper at equal milk does not depend on it: the ranking is robust to the price we chose.

2

Confirming the ration lever actually moves money

Before optimizing, a sanity check: reversing the ration raised feed cost by +122% while milk moved −0.2%. So the ration is a powerful lever on cost and a weak one on milk — exactly the asymmetry an optimizer can exploit. The feeds differ enormously in price per kilogram:

The dearest feed costs $1.00/kg; the cheapest forages $0.01/kg — a hundredfold spread.

3

Optimizing with CMA-ES, seeded at the farm's ration

CMA-ES searches the 48-dimensional ration action for maximum 7-year profit, started from the farm's configured ration so the question is posed directly: can we beat what the farm already does? Each evaluation is one full 7-year simulation in its own fresh process. Rations extreme enough to crash RuFaS's manure chemistry (3 of 48 evaluations) are scored at a large loss, so the search learns to avoid them.

4

The result, and why it happens

The optimized ration earns $2.85M over 7 years versus $2.09M for the farm's — $761,844 more (+36.4%). Feed cost falls $763,548 (39%) while milk changes -0.0%. The mechanism is one thing: in RuFaS, milk is largely insensitive to ration composition, so profit-optimal feeding shifts weight off the dear concentrates and onto the cheap forages.

What this is, and isn't

This is a static, single-lever result: one fixed ration optimized by CMA-ES, not a reinforcement-learning policy, and rations are a within-period lever. It proves the environment exposes RuFaS as a working optimization problem and returns an economically sensible, RuFaS-grounded finding — but it is the static baseline a sequential RL policy is meant to beat later. The cross-year story (fertilizer, crop rotation, manure carryover) needs those field levers wired next. The simulator's science is untouched throughout: a stepped run is identical to an unmodified run to every one of 2,843 reported quantities.
